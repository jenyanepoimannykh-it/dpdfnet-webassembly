How it works

Hush runs DPDFNet, a dual-path speech enhancement network published by CEVA, through ONNX Runtime compiled to WebAssembly. Your file is decoded by the browser, resampled to the 48 kHz the model expects, and pushed through it 20 milliseconds at a time, each channel carrying its own recurrent state.

On speech buried in noise it recovers between 8 and 15 dB, most of it where the input is worst. It shortens a reverberant room too, though far less dramatically. The network's own 40 millisecond delay is measured back out of the result, so what you download lines up sample for sample with what you put in — drop it straight back onto the video it came from.

The first visit downloads the model weights and keeps them in the browser's cache, so later visits start straight away.
